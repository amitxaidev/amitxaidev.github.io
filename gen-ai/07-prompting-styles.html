Zero-Shot vs Few-Shot vs Chain of Thought Prompting (With Python)

Zero-shot, few-shot and chain of thought prompting explained simply, with one trick question, and a Python program that builds all three prompts.

13 words fix it

You ask the AI twice and still get a wrong answer. But three words fix it. Think step by step. Here's why.

The question is a classic trick. A pen and a notebook cost a hundred and ten rupees. The notebook costs a hundred more than the pen. How much is the pen?

Your brain shouts ten rupees. So does a rushed model. Today, three prompting styles that change the answer.

quick answer: 10 rupees ✗step by step: 5 rupees ✓pen + notebook = ₹110notebook = pen + ₹100ZERO-SHOTFEW-SHOTCHAIN OF THOUGHT

2Zero-shot

First, the style you already use, without knowing its name. And for most simple tasks, it's enough.

Zero-shot means you give the task and zero examples. Translate this to Hindi. Summarise this email.

It works because the model has seen millions of similar tasks while it learned.

But it breaks when you need a very specific format, or a tricky multi-step answer. That's where the next two come in.

"Summarise this email.""Translate to Hindi."exact formatmulti-step logic

3Few-shot

Want the AI to answer in exactly your format, every time? Show it, don't tell it. That's few-shot prompting.

Give it two or three examples of question and answer. Then the real question.

The model copies the pattern. Same style, same length, same format. Like showing a new colleague two finished reports before asking for a third.

Two or three good examples beat a paragraph of instructions. And they must be correct, because it copies mistakes too.

FEW-SHOTQ: 2 apples cost ₹20. 1 apple? A: ₹10Q: 3 pens cost ₹45. 1 pen? A: ₹15Q: your question A: …correct ✓varied ✓same format ✓

4Chain of thought

Here's the one that fixes the pen question. And it's one sentence.

Chain of thought means asking the model to show its reasoning before the answer. Researchers at Google found it helps with maths and logic questions.

Watch the steps. Pen plus notebook is one ten. Notebook is pen plus a hundred. So two pens plus a hundred is one ten. Two pens is ten. One pen is five.

Each step is a small next-token guess that's easy to get right. Many small easy guesses beat one big jump.

Modern reasoning models already think before they answer. With them, you need this less. With smaller, faster models, it still helps a lot.

THINKSTEP BY STEPpen + (pen + 100) = 1102 × pen = 10pen = ₹5ONE BIG JUMP ✗MANY SMALL STEPS ✓

5All three in Python

Edit the code and press Run to try it right here, or copy it and run python3 prompt_styles.py.

prompt_styles.py · click to editDownload
# Lesson 7: zero-shot, few-shot and chain-of-thought prompts, built in Python.
# Runs without an API key: it prints the three prompts so you can paste them into any chatbot.

question = ("A pen and a notebook cost 110 rupees together. "
            "The notebook costs 100 rupees more than the pen. How much is the pen?")

# 1. Zero-shot: just the task, no examples
zero_shot = question

# 2. Few-shot: show examples of the format you want, then the real question
examples = [
    ("2 apples cost 20 rupees. How much is 1 apple?", "10 rupees"),
    ("3 pens cost 45 rupees. How much is 1 pen?", "15 rupees"),
]
few_shot = "\n\n".join(f"Q: {q}\nA: {a}" for q, a in examples) + f"\n\nQ: {question}\nA:"

# 3. Chain of thought: ask the model to reason before answering
chain_of_thought = question + "\nThink step by step, then give the final answer on the last line."

for name, prompt in [("zero-shot", zero_shot), ("few-shot", few_shot), ("chain of thought", chain_of_thought)]:
    print(f"--- {name}: {len(prompt.split())} words ---")
print()
print(chain_of_thought)

# The trap: the quick answer is 10 rupees. It's wrong.
pen = (110 - 100) / 2          # pen + (pen + 100) = 110  →  2·pen = 10
print("\ncorrect answer:", pen, "rupees")
▶ Output
A pen and a notebook cost 110 rupees...
Think step by step.

Here are all three, built in Python. Zero-shot is just the question.

Few-shot joins the examples, then adds the real question with an empty answer for the model to fill in.

Chain of thought adds one line. The full program prints all three, ready to paste into any chatbot. All the Python code is on compiled canvas dot com, ready to copy.

6Which one when

So which one should you use? Most people use only one. Here's the simple rule.

Simple, common task? Zero-shot. It's the cheapest, the shortest prompt.

Need an exact format or style? Few-shot. Show two or three examples.

Maths, logic, or several steps? Chain of thought. Ask it to reason first.

The cost. Few-shot and chain of thought use more tokens, so they're a little slower and cost a little more. Look at this graph.

Your turn. Pause. You want every product review labelled positive or negative, in one word. Which style?

Few-shot. Two examples, one positive and one negative, lock in the one-word format.

7Cheat sheet

  • Zero-shot: task only
  • Few-shot: 2-3 examples
  • Examples must be right
  • CoT: step by step
  • CoT → maths & logic
  • More tokens = more cost

8Quick quiz

1. A prompt with no examples is called what?

Zero-shot.

2. What does chain of thought ask the model to do?

Reason step by step before answering.

3. Which style is best for locking in an exact format?

Few-shot.
